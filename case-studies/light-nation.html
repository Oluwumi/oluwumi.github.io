Spotlight · The QA Dashboard

Every Call, Reviewed.

Each QA member gets a private dashboard with one tab per buddy they oversee. They read every call log in context, score it against a fixed rubric and flag what needs attention. Ratings write back to the master sheet and feed every monthly report.

  • 3 ratings from 1 to 5: Call Quality, Log Detail, Tone And Approach
  • 2 checks: Issues Addressed, Follow-Up Taken
  • 1 flag: Commend, Needs Coaching or Escalate
  • Ratings survive every refresh, so no review is ever lost

01Overview

Every first-time visitor should get two calls a week for four weeks from one assigned volunteer, a "buddy", with every call reviewed for quality.

The process ran on a shared spreadsheet that lost data. I defined the product, built it with Claude and shipped three versions, including a full rebuild after a platform suspension. It now runs across five branches and is still in use after handover.

Delivered

A decentralised follow-up platform with automatic assignment, call tracking, quality review and reporting, run from a no-code menu.

Adopted

Running at HQ and four other branches, handling about 250 new visitors a month at HQ, and still in use after handover.

Impact

Every visitor has one owner, every call is recorded in one place, and weekly and monthly reports are produced automatically.

02The Problem

03Three Versions

v1Retired

One spreadsheet with a tab for each buddy. Fixed lost notes.

Ended: still manual and hard to track.
v2Retired

Automated onboarding and emails, then a private sheet for each buddy.

Ended: reported to Google within 24 hours and suspended. Rebuilt, flagged again: every file sat on one account.
v3Live

Decentralised, rebuilt on a new account, with staged rollout.

Running at HQ and four other branches.
The Insight

Splitting the files did not split the risk. Ownership and access had to be redesigned, not just the file structure.

Google never confirmed the cause of the suspensions. My working theory was the volume of personal contact data held on one personal account.

04How v3 Works

01Intake QueueForm submissions queue for processing.
02Round-Robin AssignmentNext active buddy gets the visitor.
03Private Buddy SheetRow written to master and to the buddy's own sheet.
04Calls And NudgesEight call slots; reminders Tue, Thu, Sat.
05Resumable SyncEvery 10 minutes into master and QA.
06QA And ReportsRatings, flags, weekly and monthly reports.
  • Staged access. Sheets stay admin-only until onboarding, seven buddies a day. The onboarding email explains the sheet before access lands, and was rebuilt after it broke on Gmail mobile.
  • Call tracking. Eight call slots over four weeks, then a final status: Established, Visiting or Not Established.
  • Resumable sync. A saved progress index lets a timed-out run resume where it stopped, then refresh QA dashboards. Built because Apps Script has no edit trigger across spreadsheets.
  • Quality assurance. Three ratings from 1 to 5, two Yes, Partial or No checks, and one flag: Commend, Needs Coaching or Escalate. Assessments go out on the second and fourth Saturday.
  • Reporting. A weekly intake report, plus monthly group, team and personal reports on a two-month lag, so every visitor has completed the full four-week cycle.
  • Multi-branch. One configuration block, plus a column map that turns each branch's form wording into standard fields.

08Product Insights And Lesson

  • Build for the people who run it after you leave. A no-code admin menu is why branches still run it after handover.
  • Access design is product design. Who sees what, and in what order, decided whether the product survived.
  • Platform dependencies are product risks.
  • Real constraints beat architectural purity. I proposed stripping personal details from buddy sheets. The real requirement was a single record of every call, so they stayed.
  • Silent failures cost more than loud ones.
  • Agree definitions before building.
